\subsection{Research Questions}
\label{subsec:research_questions}

This research addresses five research questions:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{RQ1: Joint Learning Performance.}
    Does CRAM-ABSA improve joint aspect-based sentiment analysis (ABSA)
    and rating prediction compared with simple single-task and multi-task
    baselines under the same Hotel-Disjoint data split?

    \item \textbf{RQ2: Contribution of the Continuous Rating Head.}
    Does the Continuous Rating Head improve global representation and
    fine-grained opinion extraction, or does it primarily improve
    overall rating prediction?

    \item \textbf{RQ3: Conflict Attenuation Gating.}
    Does the Conflict Attenuation Gating mechanism help the model handle
    hotel reviews containing mixed or conflicting sentiments?

    \item \textbf{RQ4: Overlap F1 and Exact F1.}
    Why can the Overlap F1 score be high while the Exact F1 score remains
    low? Do the errors primarily arise from span boundaries, aspect
    mapping, or sentiment classification?

    \item \textbf{RQ5: Hotel-Disjoint Evaluation.}
    How does model performance under the Hotel-Disjoint evaluation
    setting differ from performance under a random review split?
\end{enumerate}

These questions cover joint model performance, the contribution of
individual components, fine-grained extraction errors, and
generalization to hotels not represented in the training data.
RQ1--RQ3 will be investigated through baseline comparisons and ablation
experiments. RQ4 will examine differences between overlap-based and
exact-match evaluation, while RQ5 will compare the Hotel-Disjoint and
random-split evaluation settings using clearly defined data splits and
consistent metrics.


\subsection{Key Contributions}
\label{subsec:key_contributions}

The expected contributions of this research are as follows:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{A joint learning framework for hotel reviews:}
    The study proposes CRAM-ABSA to investigate the joint learning of
    fine-grained opinion extraction and overall rating prediction.
    It examines the potential contributions of the Continuous Rating
    Head and Conflict Attenuation Gating through comparative and
    ablation experiments.

    \item \textbf{A question-answering benchmark:}
    The study develops a benchmark of 60 questions covering the six

    provide answers that address users' information needs. The
    evaluation criteria and expected answers should be defined before
    testing to support consistent comparisons.

    \item \textbf{A hotel review QA application and user interface:}
    The project develops a user interface for interacting with the
    hotel review analysis system. The application is intended to
    support aspect-oriented queries, evidence-based answers, and
    relevant follow-up questions. Its practical behavior will be
    assessed separately from the model's task-level performance.

    \item \textbf{A comparative and ablation study:}
    The research compares the proposed framework with suitable
    single-task and multi-task baselines. Ablation experiments will
    examine the contribution of individual model components, while
    application-level tests will investigate question-answering
    behavior. Any reported improvement will be supported by measured
    experimental results.
\end{itemize}

\section{Result \& Discussion}
\label{sec:resultanddiscussion}

This research focuses on real-world online hotel reviews and applies
Natural Language Processing (NLP), machine learning, and deep learning
techniques to perform fine-grained opinion extraction and overall hotel
rating prediction. The study investigates the relationship between
aspect-level opinions and overall ratings through a joint learning
framework, with the aim of improving the understanding of customer
feedback.

The scope is defined in terms of data, opinion analysis, rating
prediction, model development, evaluation, and practical application.

\subsection{Data Scope}
\label{subsec:datascope}

The research uses online hotel review data for analyzing customer
opinions and evaluating rating prediction. The data scope includes
review text, aspect and sentiment annotations where available, and
overall rating information required for the corresponding prediction
task. The availability and compatibility of these fields will determine
which records can be used for each experiment.

Data preprocessing will include identifying duplicate or invalid
records, examining missing values, and preparing review text for model
training and evaluation. Text processing will aim to preserve important
sentiment-bearing expressions, negation, and contrastive statements,
since these features can affect the interpretation of opinions across
different hotel aspects.

Exploratory data analysis will examine review length, aspect frequency,
sentiment distribution, and aspect co-occurrence where supported by the
available annotations. The resulting analysis will be used to
characterize the dataset and identify challenges relevant to the
proposed tasks.

\subsection{Opinion Analysis Scope}
\label{subsec:opinionscope}

The research focuses on extracting aspect-related opinions and
identifying the sentiment associated with each opinion. It adopts six
main aspect categories:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Service:} customer service and staff-related experiences.
    \item \textbf{Facility:} hotel rooms, physical facilities, and
    related infrastructure.
    \item \textbf{Experience:} the customer's overall stay experience.
    \item \textbf{Amenity:} available amenities and supporting services.
    \item \textbf{Loyalty:} willingness to return to or recommend a hotel.
    \item \textbf{Branding:} brand perception and related hotel identity.
\end{itemize}

These categories define the main aspect labels used in the research.
More specific topics, such as room cleanliness, staff behavior, or
hotel amenities, may be examined as sub-aspects when supported by the
data and annotation scheme.

The system aims to preserve different opinions expressed within the
same review rather than assigning a single sentiment label to the
entire document. For example, a review may describe a room positively
while expressing dissatisfaction with staff service. Such cases
illustrate the need to distinguish sentiment associated with different
aspects.

The evaluation will consider the relevant aspect, sentiment, and
opinion-span predictions according to the annotation format and the
defined evaluation protocol.

\subsection{Rating Prediction Scope}
\label{subsec:ratingscope}

The second major task is to predict the overall rating associated with
a hotel review. The research will investigate whether review text and
information derived from fine-grained opinions can provide useful
signals for estimating the reviewer's overall assessment.

The study will compare appropriate model configurations to examine
whether incorporating aspect-level information provides additional
value beyond using review text alone. The rating representation,
prediction target, and evaluation procedure will be defined consistently
with the rating information available in the dataset.

The proposed Continuous Rating Head is intended to support continuous
rating prediction and to investigate how global rating information
can contribute to fine-grained opinion extraction. Its implementation
and effectiveness will be examined experimentally rather than assumed
in advance.

\subsection{Model Scope}
\label{subsec:modelscope}

The research considers Transformer-based language models for contextual
text representation and fine-grained opinion analysis. BERT
\cite{devlin2019bert} provides a relevant foundation for representing
the linguistic context of hotel reviews.

The proposed CRAM-ABSA framework investigates joint learning between
fine-grained opinion extraction and overall rating prediction. It
includes two proposed components:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Continuous Rating Head:} designed to represent
    overall rating information and investigate its contribution to
    fine-grained opinion extraction.
    \item \textbf{Conflict Attenuation Gating:} designed to regulate
    the influence of conflicting sentiment information across
    different aspects during joint learning.
\end{itemize}

The experimental comparison will include the following model settings,
subject to the availability of compatible implementations and training
configurations:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item A single-task model for fine-grained opinion extraction.
    \item A single-task model for overall rating prediction.
    \item A simple multi-task learning baseline.
    \item The proposed CRAM-ABSA framework.
\end{enumerate}

The comparison will investigate whether joint learning and the proposed
components provide measurable benefits over the selected baselines.
The final model configurations and training procedures will be
documented in the methodology and experimental setup.

\subsection{Evaluation Scope}
\label{subsec:evaluationscope}

Fine-grained opinion extraction will be evaluated using Precision,
Recall, and F1-score, with the calculation based on the relevant
prediction unit and annotation scheme.

\begin{equation}
Precision =
\frac{TP}{TP+FP}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
Recall =
\frac{TP}{TP+FN}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
F1 =
2 \times
\frac{Precision \times Recall}
{Precision + Recall}
\end{equation}

For continuous overall rating prediction, the research will use
Mean Absolute Error (MAE), Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE), and the
coefficient of determination ($R^2$).

\begin{equation}
MAE =
\frac{1}{n}
\sum_{i=1}^{n}
|y_i-\hat{y}_i|
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
RMSE =
\sqrt{
\frac{1}{n}
\sum_{i=1}^{n}
(y_i-\hat{y}_i)^2
}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
R^2 =
1 -
\frac{
\sum_{i=1}^{n}(y_i-\hat{y}_i)^2
}{
\sum_{i=1}^{n}(y_i-\bar{y})^2
}
\end{equation}

Here, $y_i$ denotes the reference rating, $\hat{y}_i$ denotes the
predicted rating, $\bar{y}$ denotes the mean reference rating, and
$n$ denotes the number of evaluated samples.

Ablation experiments will examine the individual contributions of
the Continuous Rating Head and Conflict Attenuation Gating mechanism.
The experimental protocol will keep the evaluation data and relevant
training conditions consistent across compared configurations wherever
possible.

\subsection{Application Scope}
\label{subsec:applicationscope}

The research also considers the use of aspect-level opinions and
overall rating information in a hotel review question-answering
application. The intended system can support questions about specific
dimensions of hotel experiences and provide responses based on
relevant review evidence where available.

The application serves as a practical context for the research.
However, the main research focus remains the analysis of hotel reviews,
fine-grained opinion extraction, overall rating prediction, and the
evaluation of the proposed joint learning approach. Application-level
responses will not be treated as evidence of model effectiveness
without a separate evaluation.

\subsection{Out of Scope}
\label{subsec:outscope}

The following areas are outside the scope of this research:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Hotel price optimization and dynamic pricing.
    \item Hotel booking and reservation management.
    \item Real-time hotel operations and resource management.
    \item Direct control of hotel management processes.
    \item Forecasting hotel market prices independently of review
    analysis and rating prediction.
\end{itemize}

The research is limited to hotel review understanding, fine-grained
opinion extraction, overall rating prediction, and the investigation
of joint learning methods for these tasks.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:feasibility}

This research is feasible because hotel review data and relevant annotation
resources are available for developing and evaluating the proposed model.
The project already has an English hotel review dataset in JSON format
containing review text and annotated aspects and sentiments. These resources
provide an initial basis for data analysis and fine-grained opinion
extraction. However, the availability and consistency of overall rating
labels must be checked before conducting rating prediction experiments.

The required technologies are also accessible. Python and libraries such as
Pandas, NumPy, Scikit-learn, PyTorch, and Hugging Face Transformers can
support data preprocessing, exploratory data analysis, model development,
training, and evaluation. These tools also allow the proposed joint learning
framework to be compared with single-task and multi-task learning baselines.

The project can be developed on a personal computer for data inspection,
preprocessing, and initial experiments. Training Transformer-based models
may require additional computing resources, depending on the model size,
batch size, and dataset configuration. Therefore, a suitable GPU or
cloud-based computing resource may be used when necessary.

The research can be organized into manageable stages, including data
preparation, exploratory analysis, baseline implementation, joint model
development, experimental evaluation, and system integration. Team members
can work in parallel on data processing, model development, evaluation, and
documentation. This division of work supports progress within the proposed
schedule while allowing sufficient time for debugging and experimental
validation.

Overall, the project is considered feasible because its initial dataset,
software tools, and development workflow are available. Nevertheless, the
final feasibility of the rating prediction component depends on the
availability of compatible rating labels, while the proposed model
improvements must be validated through experiments rather than assumed in advance.

\section{Approach and Method}
\label{sec:method}

This research follows a pipeline that combines Natural Language Processing
(NLP), fine-grained opinion extraction, and overall rating prediction.
The proposed CRAM-ABSA framework aims to learn aspect-level opinions and
overall ratings jointly. The experiments will compare the proposed
framework with single-task and multi-task learning baselines to evaluate
the contribution of each component.

\subsection{Step 1: Data Collection and Preprocessing}

The study will use hotel review data containing review text and aspect-level
annotations. The available English dataset, \texttt{TripAdvisor\_EN.json},
contains review records with annotated aspects and sentiments. Before
training, the data will be checked for duplicate records, missing text,
invalid annotations, and inconsistent labels.

Text preprocessing will include basic normalization and tokenization where
required by the selected model. Negation words and contrastive expressions,
such as ``not'' and ``but'', will be preserved because they can change the
meaning or polarity of an opinion. Aspect and sentiment annotations will be
checked against their corresponding text spans to maintain alignment between
the original review and its labels.

Overall rating prediction requires a compatible rating label for each
review used in the experiment. Therefore, rating availability and label
consistency will be verified before constructing the rating prediction
dataset. If the aspect annotations and rating labels come from different
sources, their compatibility must be established before they are combined.

\subsection{Step 3: Fine-Grained Opinion Extraction}

The model will identify hotel aspects and their associated sentiments from
each review. The study uses six main aspect categories:
\textit{Service}, \textit{Facility}, \textit{Experience},
\textit{Amenity}, \textit{Loyalty}, and \textit{Branding}. The sentiment
labels are \textit{Positive}, \textit{Negative}, and \textit{Neutral},
following the available annotation scheme.

For example, the review ``The room was clean but the staff were
unfriendly'' expresses different opinions about different parts of the
hotel experience. The model should preserve this difference rather than
assigning a single sentiment to the entire review. The final aspect labels
must follow the dataset's annotation rules.

A Transformer-based model will be used to learn contextual representations
of review text and predict aspect and sentiment information. The proposed
joint model will be evaluated against suitable single-task and multi-task
baselines. Opinion extraction performance will be measured using
Precision, Recall, and F1-score. Where span annotations are available,
span-level evaluation will be used with a clearly defined matching
criterion.

\FloatBarrier

\subsection{Step 4: Overall Rating Prediction}

The study will investigate whether the model can predict an overall hotel
review rating from textual information and, where appropriate, extracted
aspect-level opinions. The proposed CRAM-ABSA framework includes a rating
prediction component intended to learn the relationship between detailed
opinions and the overall evaluation expressed in a review.

The rating prediction target and output layer will be selected according
to the available rating labels. If the target is a continuous numerical
score, the model will be evaluated using Mean Absolute Error (MAE), Root
Mean Squared Error (RMSE), and the coefficient of determination
($R^2$). If the available labels are ordinal rating categories, the
prediction setup and evaluation protocol will be adjusted to match that
target.

The experiments will compare a text-based rating prediction baseline with
models that incorporate aspect-level opinion information. This comparison
will help determine whether fine-grained opinions provide useful
information for predicting overall ratings.

\FloatBarrier

\subsection{Step 5: Joint Learning and Ablation Studies}

The proposed CRAM-ABSA framework will be compared with single-task models
and a suitable multi-task learning baseline. The comparison will examine
both aspect-level opinion extraction and overall rating prediction, using
consistent data splits and evaluation procedures.

The study will investigate three main questions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Joint learning:} Does learning aspect-level opinions and
    overall ratings together improve performance compared with training
    separate task-specific models?

    \item \textbf{Rating information:} Does incorporating overall rating
    supervision help the model extract more accurate aspect-level opinions
    or improve rating prediction?

    \item \textbf{Conflict attenuation:} Does the proposed
    Conflict Attenuation Gating mechanism help the model handle reviews
    that contain different or conflicting opinions across aspects?
\end{itemize}

Ablation experiments will be conducted by removing or disabling selected
components of the proposed framework while keeping the remaining
experimental settings as consistent as possible. The results will be used
to assess the contribution of each component. Any improvement will be
reported only when it is supported by the experimental results.

\subsection{Step 6: Application Integration}

The research outputs may be integrated into a hotel review analysis
application or chatbot. The application will allow users to ask questions
about hotel services and receive concise answers based on the available
reviews, aspect-level opinions, and rating information.

The application will retrieve relevant review evidence and summarize
opinions across the six main aspect categories. Its responses should
reflect the available evidence and distinguish positive, negative, and
neutral feedback where supported by the data. The application will be
evaluated as a demonstration of the research workflow; its performance
will not be treated as proof of model effectiveness without supporting
experimental results.

\section{Expected Results}
\label{sec:expected}

This research is expected to develop and evaluate CRAM-ABSA, a joint
learning framework that combines fine-grained aspect-based sentiment
analysis with overall hotel rating prediction. The framework will be
designed to analyze hotel reviews at both the aspect level and the overall
rating level.

The study is expected to provide a systematic comparison between the
proposed framework, single-task models, and a simple multi-task learning
baseline. Performance will be evaluated using appropriate metrics for
aspect-level opinion extraction and overall rating prediction. The
experimental results will determine whether joint learning provides
advantages over the baseline approaches.

The research is also expected to provide evidence about the contribution
of overall rating information to fine-grained opinion extraction. In
addition, the study will investigate whether the proposed Conflict
Attenuation Gating mechanism helps the model handle reviews containing
different or conflicting sentiments across hotel aspects.

From a methodological perspective, the study will evaluate the proposed
integration of the Continuous Rating Head and Conflict Attenuation Gating
through comparative and ablation experiments. These experiments will
examine the contribution of individual components and help answer the
research questions. The effectiveness of each component will be determined
from the experimental results rather than assumed in advance.

From a practical perspective, the research may support the development of
a hotel review analysis application or chatbot. The system is expected to
summarize customer opinions across six main aspect categories:
\textit{Service}, \textit{Facility}, \textit{Experience},
\textit{Amenity}, \textit{Loyalty}, and \textit{Branding}. Where
compatible rating data are available, it may also provide predicted
overall ratings. These outputs could help users understand customer
feedback more clearly and make better-informed hotel-related decisions.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This research proposes CRAM-ABSA, a joint learning framework for fine-grained
opinion extraction and overall hotel rating prediction from real-world hotel
reviews. The framework aims to learn aspect-level opinions together with
overall rating information, providing a more comprehensive understanding of
customer feedback than analyzing these tasks independently.

The study will compare the proposed framework with single-task and
multi-task learning baselines to evaluate the potential benefits of joint
learning. It will also investigate the contributions of the Continuous
Rating Head and Conflict Attenuation Gating through comparative experiments
and ablation studies. The results will help determine whether these
components improve model performance and how overall rating information
relates to aspect-level opinion extraction.

Ultimately, this research aims to support the development of hotel review
analysis systems that summarize customer opinions across six main aspects:
Service, Facility, Experience, Amenity, Loyalty, and Branding. By combining
fine-grained opinions with overall rating information, the proposed approach
may help users better understand hotel feedback and make more informed
decisions. The effectiveness of the framework will be determined through
experimental evaluation.
